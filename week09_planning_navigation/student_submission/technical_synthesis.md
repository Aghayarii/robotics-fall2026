Throughout these three missions, I learned that a good robot route is not just about finding the shortest path. It also needs to be efficient, reliable, and safe for people sharing the same space.
In Mission 1, BFS and A* both found a shortest path of 13.5 meters on the open map. However, A* expanded only 700 nodes compared to 928 for BFS. This showed me how using a heuristic can make searching more efficient without changing the path length.
In Mission 2, I learned that faster planning does not always mean faster navigation. On the constrained map, BFS finished in 89.17 seconds, while A* took 89.68 seconds, even though both traveled 23.90 meters. Nav2 performed better overall, completing the open map in 35.50 seconds and the constrained map in 73.36 seconds. This showed how turns, obstacles, and the robot's ability to follow a path affect real execution.
Mission 3 showed why personal space matters. For A*, the social_2 setting increased minimum clearance from 0.733 m to 0.812 m and reduced personal-space exposure from 1.5 seconds to zero. However, stronger repulsion also caused oscillation in the constrained-map preview.
Overall, I think successful navigation means reaching the destination while balancing efficiency, reliability, and human comfort. The clearance, speed, and tracking values are estimates from the simulation, not perfect measurements of real-world safety. The experiments also used stationary people, so more testing would be needed to understand behavior around moving people.
