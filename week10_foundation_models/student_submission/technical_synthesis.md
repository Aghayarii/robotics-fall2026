From these three missions, I learned that giving clear instructions to an AI model is important, but it does not always mean the robot will perform the task correctly.
In Mission 1, the model initially assumed the cup was empty without actually inspecting it. After I improved my request, the model explained that it would use its camera to inspect the cup and say if it was unsure. This showed me that more specific instructions can help reduce incorrect assumptions.
In Mission 2, the model first suggested using a measuring tape without knowing what the robot needed to do. When I explained that the goal was to draw a circle on wood, the model correctly suggested a compass, which was visible in the picture. This showed how providing the task and using visual information can improve the answer.
In Mission 3, even with detailed instructions, the robot failed to reach the target. With my improved prompt, it made only 6 successful moves, had 22 blocked moves, and stopped 22 times. The simulator prevented it from moving through walls, but the model still kept choosing incorrect actions. Overall, I learned that AI models can understand instructions and suggest useful actions, but they still have limitations. Clear prompts, visual evidence, and action checks are all important, but the model also needs to make better decisions when its first plan does not work.
